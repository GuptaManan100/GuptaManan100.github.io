# 📚 [_Anxious People_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/49127718-anxious-people)

**Genre:** Fiction, Contemporary, Humor
**Originally Published:** 2019

---

## 💭 Quick Summary & Thoughts

This book had been on my TBR for a while, and quite a few of my friends who read it had rated it highly. However, that didn't match my own experience. The book is positioned as a humorous story, but it isn't all that funny. Maybe it's just me. I find it hard to find humour in written content, as opposed to stand-up comedy, but the book didn't land with me the way the author probably intended. It's still a fun, quirky story, and worth a read as a palate cleanser if you're looking for a fast one.

It's a story about a bank robbery, the hostage situation that follows, and the investigation after that. That description might make you think it's an exciting detective book, but it's not. It's the exact opposite. It's a very unserious book, full of misunderstandings, lies, and events engineered to add surprise and humour.

Overall, it was a pleasant read. Some parts were good, some not as much, but it's a decent airplane read nonetheless.

Like I already said, I didn't particularly enjoy the book the way the author intended, but I liked it for very different reasons, maybe ones the author didn't intend 😆. The humour largely didn't land on me, and at best I found myself smiling, not really laughing. I also hate it when books show events that are almost impossible to imagine in a real-life situation. For example, mistaking a box of lights for a bomb. That would almost never happen. I would like to imagine that no policemen in the world are that incompetent.

With the annoying part out of the way, I'll tell you why I did like the book. Even though it isn't that long, and there are quite a few hostages, the author has done a brilliant job of giving each one of them a real character, and their stories are so much more interesting to think about.

Let's talk about Zara, the banker. She feels responsible for the death of the man who jumped from the bridge, and her struggle with her identity, being a banker while not wanting to be one, really shows. Even after all these years, she hasn't moved on from that incident. Her wanting to view apartments overlooking the bridge isn't just curiosity; it's also guilt, a need to learn more about the ordinary people who live in these places and what she can do to make up for her mistakes.

Then there's Nadia, the therapist herself, and I love how all the characters' stories tie together: Jack, the policeman, saved her on that bridge and found his calling because of that incident.

The married couple's story is just as great. Roger put his career on hold so Anna-Lena could move hers along. And then Anna-Lena does whatever she can, including hiring a stage actor, to get Roger what he wants and make him feel like a provider. I'm a sucker for romantic stories like these, and this one made me smile and feel all happy inside.

And finally, there's Estelle, and how she met someone while she was already married. She didn't get involved physically, but they exchanged books. It's what my generation calls emotional cheating these days. I can't really understand why someone would let their mind even go there when they're already in a committed relationship, so this one didn't land as strongly for me.
