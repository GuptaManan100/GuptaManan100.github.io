# 📚 [_Never Let Me Go_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6334.Never_Let_Me_Go)

**Genre:** Fiction, Science Fiction, Literary Fiction, Dystopia
**Originally Published:** 2005

---

## 💭 Quick Summary & Thoughts

Having read the book, I can understand what Kazuo Ishiguro was aiming for: a slow-paced book where the twist doesn't reveal itself until much later. However, there was enough foreshadowing that I picked up on it early, so the hard-hitting moment didn't land as such. I knew where the book was headed, and it never strayed from that path. It's advertised as sci-fi, but it's really more of a slice-of-life novel, with the sci-fi element only coming in much later. It does make you think about some very relevant topics, which I can't name without giving the plot away. But it isn't as fun a read as some of his other books.

I'm generally not a fan of slow-paced books where the characters just drift through life. I like reading books where something extraordinary happens. Our own lives are banal enough that I don't want to read about some kids growing up and discovering the challenges of adulthood. Those who appreciate that kind of book might find this one a lot more appealing than I did.

One thing is absolutely crucial, though: if you do decide to read this book, don't listen to or read anything else about it until you're done. It will completely spoil the experience and colour your opinion in a light the author actively tries to avoid.

I've been backlogged on my book reviews, so I'm writing this one after we'd already discussed the book in book club. We brought up multiple talking points, but the sentiment I expressed above was largely unanimous.

We discussed why Kathy and the rest didn't just leave. They had cars and enough autonomy to basically just disappear: go live in some suburb under an assumed identity and never show up for donations. We concluded it could be because of how they were brought up, having been groomed their whole lives and told that donations were the only path for them to follow.

Another thing I found a little weird was why Madame curated only the best artwork. If you want to convince the world that cloned children are children too, wouldn't you want to show all of their artwork, faults and everything? Why only display the best? Isn't that just going to add fuel to the fire that the clones are superhuman and better than ordinary kids?

We also talked a little about how the rumour about deferrals started. There really wasn't a concrete explanation in the book, but we figured it just started somewhere and got passed along from seniors to juniors, and kept going that way. Ruth's deathbed confession didn't feel sincere to any of us; it felt more like her cleansing her conscience in her final moments. And the whole "lost corner" thing and the trip to Norfolk, I didn't understand how any of that was relevant to the plot.

And finally, the thing I caught onto very early that kinda gave away the plot: none of the kids at Hailsham ever talked about their parents. The kids talked about art, other kids, guardians, but never parents. To me that was a clear giveaway that they didn't have any, so they were either adopted or cloned. I bet my money on the latter since it was a Kazuo Ishiguro book, and I am rarely wrong 😄
