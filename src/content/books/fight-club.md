# 📚 [_Fight Club_](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/36236124-fight-club)

**Genre:** Fiction, Thriller, Contemporary, Classics
**Originally Published:** 1996

---

## 💭 Quick Summary & Thoughts

Chuck Palahniuk's books are always edgy, extraordinary, and almost certainly gruesome, at least in my experience. The level of gruesome varies, but you can expect at least some unsavoury content. This book doesn't rise to the level of _Haunted_, but consider this your trigger warning: if you don't want to read content like that, stay away from this book.

I hate reading run-of-the-mill content, and I chase uniqueness in every book I read. Chuck always delivers on that. I might not like the book, but it's almost always nothing like anything I've read before. That's the perfect way to describe this one too. It's a weird story, and I don't think it's plausible enough to happen in real life, but I hadn't read anything like it before.

There's a plot twist in the book that's supposed to come as a big revelation, but unfortunately I guessed it in the first few pages, so it didn't really deliver the shock-and-awe moment the author probably wanted. But that's the curse of reading as much as I do. The more I read, the less chance a book has of surprising me, and I keep getting better at recognizing the foreshadowing and reading between the lines.

There's also a movie of the same name, starring Brad Pitt and Edward Norton. It largely does a good job of staying true to the book, except for the ending, and even that difference isn't very significant. So if you don't want to read the book, you can just watch the movie.

Overall, it was kinda what I expected, given its Goodreads rating and knowing the author the way I do. So I can't say I'm disappointed, though I probably would have had more fun if I hadn't caught onto the twist so early.

Let me start off with this: "I know this because Tyler knows this." That line was the dead giveaway for me. It's not "I know this because Tyler told me." No, it's "I know this because Tyler knows this." Once or twice, I would have brushed it off as a turn of phrase, but the statement is repeated so many times that I figured it had to be a split personality. From that point on, I read the book in that light, with the narrator and Tyler being the same person. That meant his interactions with Marla weren't that weird to me; I was already in on the secret. I understood immediately why Tyler told the narrator not to talk to Marla about him. It was his subconscious mind not wanting Marla to find out.

Starting fight club and establishing its members, all of that was fine, but the whole business of assignments, and then guns and bombs, is where the edginess kicks in. Even the soap-making from liposuction fat! Ewwwww! And then there's the explicit and gory content, with the cutting off of balls and what not. Like I said before, if it's a Chuck Palahniuk book, you'd better be ready for anything, because he throws curveball after curveball!

I don't think his books are deep enough to carry a hidden meaning about the suppressed rage most people carry, especially against the rich. But if you think about it, it is a pretty good description of it, like the way the waiters behave, peeing in the soup and so on. That's another reason I give trigger warnings: it can give you trust issues. When you receive your bowl of soup at a restaurant, can you really be sure nobody peed into it?
